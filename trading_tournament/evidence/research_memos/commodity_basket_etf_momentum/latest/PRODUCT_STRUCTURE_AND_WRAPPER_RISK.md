# Product Structure And Wrapper Risk

1. Which reviewed products are ETFs, ETNs, or other wrappers?

   PDBC and COMT appear to be ETF wrappers. DBC, GSG, and USCI appear to be commodity pool/trust/fund wrappers rather than simple equity ETFs. This was not revalidated against official current documents in this task.

2. Which are futures-based commodity products?

   All reviewed products likely obtain commodity exposure through futures-linked indexes or futures-based strategies inside the wrapper. The project must model wrapper price series only and must not claim direct futures strategy evidence.

3. Which may issue K-1s or have partnership/tax complexity?

   DBC and USCI likely carry K-1/commodity-pool tax complexity. GSG may also require product-specific tax review. PDBC is commonly identified as a no-K-1 commodity ETF, but this must be verified from official documents before acquisition.

4. Which have ETN credit-risk or issuer-risk concerns?

   None of the reviewed products is treated as an ETN from local evidence, but wrapper and issuer-risk review remains required. If a future symbol is an ETN, it must be separately gated for issuer credit risk.

5. Which are broad commodity baskets versus narrow commodity exposure?

   All five are broad commodity basket candidates, though methodology can tilt exposure materially by sector. GSG may be energy-heavy depending S&P GSCI methodology; this needs official confirmation.

6. Which product structures are acceptable for research_sample only?

   PDBC and COMT look most acceptable for future research_sample consideration after data/terms/product gates. DBC, GSG, and USCI may still be reviewed, but require stronger wrapper/tax/risk warnings.

7. Which should be rejected or deferred due to wrapper risk?

   None is rejected from product review now. DBC, GSG, and USCI are higher-risk wrappers and should be deferred from implementation until official structure/tax/methodology details are confirmed.

8. What product-specific warnings must appear in future outputs?

   Future outputs must label evidence as commodity wrapper evidence, not direct futures evidence. They must warn about roll yield, collateral treatment, product methodology changes, fund closure, tax complexity, liquidity/spread risk, and possible duplicated GLD/top2 commodity exposure.
