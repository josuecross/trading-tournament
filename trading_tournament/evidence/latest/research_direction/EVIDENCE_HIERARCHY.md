# Evidence Hierarchy

Evidence quality matters more than the number of files produced.

## Highest Priority

1. Rolling target-before-stop rate.
2. Stress-slippage survival.
3. Benchmark-relative performance.
4. Max drawdown / stop-hit behavior.
5. Median and worst rolling-window outcome.
6. Time-to-target.

These are essential because they directly address whether the +$300/+400 challenge target is plausible before the -$600 risk budget is violated.

## Useful Supporting Evidence

7. Strategy contribution.
8. Symbol concentration.
9. Data quality and inception effects.

These help explain whether results come from broad strategy behavior, a few lucky trades, one symbol, or data artifacts.

## Secondary Evidence

10. Full-period result.
11. CAGR.
12. Sharpe / Sortino.

These are useful context, but they can mislead if used alone. A strategy can have an attractive full-period result and still fail most short challenge windows.

## Often Misleading Without Context

13. Profit factor.
14. Win rate.
15. R-multiple.

Profit factor can hide concentration. Win rate can hide large losses. R-multiple can mislead if actual risk utilization is inconsistent or if tiny-risk trades create exaggerated multiples.

## Explicit Warnings

- Full-period final equity can mislead.
- Target reached = true can mislead without time-to-target.
- R-multiple can mislead if risk utilization is inconsistent.
- More evidence files do not equal stronger evidence.
- More strategies do not equal diversification.
- More complex models do not imply more reliable results.

The strongest evidence is boring, repeatable, stress-tested, benchmark-aware, and hard to explain away.
