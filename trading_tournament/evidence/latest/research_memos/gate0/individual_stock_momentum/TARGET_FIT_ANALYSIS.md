# Target Fit Analysis

- +$300 from $3,000 = +10%.
- +$400 from $3,000 = +13.3%.
- -$600 risk budget = -20%.

Individual stocks can theoretically hit +10% faster than ETFs because single names have higher idiosyncratic volatility and larger cross-sectional dispersion. A strong stock winner can contribute more to a small account than a diversified ETF.

That same volatility increases stop risk. Earnings gaps, single-name news, and market selloffs can push equity toward the -20% stop faster than an ETF portfolio.

Diversification may reduce target probability because gains are spread across more names. Concentration may increase target probability but also increases drawdown and gap risk. A small account creates tension between enough positions for diversification and enough concentration to matter.

Large-cap liquid stocks are more realistic than microcaps because spreads, volume, and borrow constraints are more manageable. Microcaps, low-float stocks, penny stocks, and illiquid names may appear attractive in historical data but are not suitable for this project.

The target should be evaluated by rolling target-before-stop rate. One lucky winner is not enough. A stock momentum system must show repeatable target-before-stop behavior, stress-cost survival, benchmark improvement, and acceptable concentration.

Preliminary conclusion: individual stock momentum may be worth Gate 1 research, but not implementation yet.
