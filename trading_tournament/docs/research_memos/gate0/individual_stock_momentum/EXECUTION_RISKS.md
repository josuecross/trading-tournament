# Execution Risks

Individual stocks can be harder to model than ETFs because costs and gaps vary widely by name and regime.

## Main Risks

- Wider spreads than ETFs: many stocks have less consistent liquidity than SPY, QQQ, or major ETFs.
- Slippage: modeled open prices may not be achievable, especially after strong momentum signals.
- Gaps: stops may be bypassed at the open.
- Earnings jumps: earnings can create large overnight moves in either direction.
- Halts: single-name news can halt trading and break ordinary stop assumptions.
- Liquidity holes: names that look tradable on average may be thin during stress.
- Market open fills: opening auctions can be noisy and costly.
- Stop execution realism: daily bars cannot fully model stop queue, spread, or gap behavior.
- Fractional share assumptions: fractional shares may not reflect all brokers or order types.
- Notional caps: small accounts can still become concentrated in a few names.
- Small account sizing: $3,000 limits diversification and can magnify idiosyncratic risk.
- Correlation during selloffs: stocks can become highly correlated when risk-off.
- Borrow/short constraints: any future shorting idea requires borrow availability, locate cost, and short-sale rules.

## Conclusion

Long-only liquid large-cap stocks may be feasible later. Shorting, low-float stocks, penny stocks, and illiquid stocks should be rejected for now.
